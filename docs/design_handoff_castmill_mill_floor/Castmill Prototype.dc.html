Shipping Blazor at Scale: what actually moved the numbers

Every team we talk to starts the same way: the app works, the demo lands, and then twenty people open it at once. What follows is not a rewrite. In this session, Maya walked through four changes that each cost less than a day and moved a number we could point at.

Prerender the shell, not the page

First paint went from 4.2 seconds to 900 milliseconds by moving the shell to prerender — a single boundary change. Nothing about the component tree moved; the wait simply stopped being the user's problem.

Virtualize what you measured

The grid was eighty percent of the frame budget before virtualization. The lesson was not "virtualize everything" but "measure first, then virtualize the thing that showed up."
